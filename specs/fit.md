## 1. Notation

| symbol | meaning |
|---|---|
| (t_i, y_i), i = 1 … N | observations used in the fit (after exclusions, FIT-ERR-01) |
| θ = (θ_1 … θ_P) | model parameters being estimated; P of them (fixed parameters are not counted) |
| ŷ_i = f(t_i; θ) | model prediction (`specs/models.md`) |
| w_i | weight of observation i (FIT-WGT-01) |
| r_i | residual y_i − ŷ_i; weighted residual √w_i·r_i |
| WRSS(θ) | weighted residual sum of squares Σ w_i·r_i² (the objective) |
| J | weighted Jacobian, J_ij = √w_i·∂f(t_i; θ)/∂θ_j (N × P) |
| DF | degrees of freedom N − P |
| S² = WRSS/DF, S | residual variance and its square root (the standard error of the fit) |
| Cov | variance–covariance matrix of the estimates, S²·(JᵀJ)⁻¹ |

## 2. Objective and weights

### FIT-OBJ-01 Objective
Minimise WRSS(θ) = Σ w_i·(y_i − f(t_i; θ))² over the free parameters, subject to bounds (FIT-BND-01). The estimate is a minimiser; local minima are possible (non-linear model), so the result is "a" minimum reached from the initial estimates.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the minimiser of every one of the 90 reference fits (estimates 1e-4 relative, WRSS 1e-6 relative), E-10.
- Sources: S-28 (weighted non-linear least squares, book level); S-23, S-24, S-25; D.

### FIT-DAT-01 Observations a fit reads
A fit reads the rows of one subject that have a time and a concentration. Rows with a missing concentration are left out and counted (`n_missing`). Rows whose concentration was written below the limit of quantification (`specs/nca.md` NCA-DAT-05c) are left out too and counted apart (`n_blq`): they hold 0 only as a BLQ marker, and fitting that 0 as a measured value would pull the curve down. A 0 written as a number is a measured value and stays in (with `inv_y` or `inv_y2` it then cannot be weighted, FIT-WGT-02). The initial estimates read the same rows.
- Status: `assumed` (design choice of task T-097: BLQ observations are discarded, the simplest of the usual methods; censored likelihoods are out of scope for least squares)
- Tested by: `crates/caladrius-engine/src/tests.rs` (`a_fit_leaves_blq_rows_out_and_counts_them`).

### FIT-WGT-01 Weighting schemes
Five schemes, selectable by name:

| id | weight w_i | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `uniform` | 1 | ordinary least squares |
| `inv_y` | 1/y_i | variance proportional to the observed value |
| `inv_y2` | 1/y_i² | constant coefficient of variation, on the observed values |
| `inv_yhat` | 1/ŷ_i | as `inv_y`, on the predicted values |
| `inv_yhat2` | 1/ŷ_i² | as `inv_y2`, on the predicted values |

Uniform weighting on the observed values is the default (`AGENTS.md` section 6). The weights do not depend on the parameters for the first three schemes; for the last two they do (FIT-WGT-03). Weights are used as given and are not rescaled; the estimates and the covariance matrix are unchanged if all weights are multiplied by the same positive constant, but WRSS, S² and the information criteria change (property used as a test, example F2).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: all five weightings on `fit_theoph_*` (12 subjects each), `fit_indometh_*` (6 subjects each) and `fit_spec_*` (E-10). The default (uniform) and the non-rescaling of weights are pinned by the defaults test.
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` sections 4 and 6 (the five schemes and the default); S-28 (weighted least squares, book level); D (invariance: Cov = S²·(JᵀJ)⁻¹, with S² and (JᵀJ)⁻¹ scaling by c and 1/c).

### FIT-WGT-02 Observed values that cannot be weighted
With `inv_y` or `inv_y2` an observation with y ≤ 0, or so small that its weight is not finite (below about 1e-154 for `inv_y2`), has an undefined or infinite weight. The run stops before fitting with an error (`UnweightableObservation`: "zero, negative, or too small to weight") that names the observations (time and value) and offers two ways out: exclude them (they stay in the table with the reason `zero_weight` and are not counted in N), or choose another scheme. Nothing is replaced by a tiny number silently.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the test "observed-value weights refuse y ≤ 0" of `oracle_fit.rs` (E-10); the too-small case by an engine unit test (E-11).
- Sources: golden rule 6 of `AGENTS.md` (zero or negative concentrations give a readable error); design choice.

### FIT-WGT-03 Predicted-value weights
For `inv_yhat` and `inv_yhat2` the weights are recomputed from the current predictions at the start of every iteration and are treated as constants when the derivatives are formed (iteratively reweighted least squares); the final statistics use the weights at the solution. The target of such a fit is therefore a fixed point θ with Jᵀ·W(θ)·r(θ) = 0, W from the predictions at θ, which is not the minimiser of Σ r²/ŷ² with the weight differentiated too (open item OF-04, untested by design; for the proportional error model the maximum-likelihood fit of FIT-ML-12 is the true minimiser and is shown to differ). A trial point with a prediction ŷ_i ≤ 0 is rejected like a step that does not decrease WRSS (FIT-ALG-03). An observation whose prediction is exactly 0 at every parameter value cannot carry a predicted-value weight: the caller leaves it out, as the oracle does for the Theoph fits (their sample at t = 0 is not used). Which rows have that property is the rule of FIT-ML-13c: on a route that is not a bolus, the row at the dose time t = 0 (the first-order oral model at t = 0, for one), and on a route with a lag also a row at t > 0 as long as a trial value of tlag can reach that time (the weight is then undefined at that trial point, and the trial is rejected as above). Source: the orchestrator's rule on the T-149 thread, 2026-11-07.

What the oracle established about these fits (E-10; the first version of the Indometh cases was wrong and was corrected by card T-018):
- On Theoph all five weightings settle by the plain "refit with frozen weights, then reweight" iteration.
- On Indometh (a one-compartment model fitted to a biexponential profile, a poor but legitimate fit) the plain iteration cycles between two estimates for `inv_yhat2` on 5 of the 6 subjects and does not settle for `inv_yhat`. The fixed points exist nevertheless, one per subject and scheme over a grid of starts (V from 1 to 300, k from 0.02 to 10), found by root finding on the stationarity equations and confirmed in 200-bit arithmetic. `stats::nls` fails to converge for 5 of the 6 `inv_yhat2` subjects, which is why the references come from root finding there.
- Points that look like solutions but are not: the first oracle accepted, for `inv_yhat` subjects 1, 3, 5 and 6, estimates with V near 0.004, k near 28 and WRSS near 1e89, because its stationarity test was divided by a quantity containing the weights, which explodes there. An acceptance test must be scale-free (FIT-WGT-04).
- The engine reaches the genuine fixed points by damped steps (FIT-ALG-04, FIT-CNV-05).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: `fit_theoph_inv_yhat`, `fit_theoph_inv_yhat2` (12 subjects, solved by iteration), `fit_indometh_inv_yhat` (6 subjects, all by root finding), `fit_indometh_inv_yhat2` (subject 2 by iteration, the other five by root finding), `fit_spec_inv_yhat`, `fit_spec_inv_yhat2`; the engine matches all of them at the 1e-4 and 1e-6 tolerances (E-10, E-11). Worked example F5.
- Sources: S-28 (iterative reweighting, book level); design choice; E-10, E-11 (cards T-009, T-018, T-011a).

### FIT-WGT-04 How a predicted-weight solution is verified
A candidate (V, k, …) is a fixed point when the stationarity sums Σ_i w_i·r_i·∂f_i/∂θ_j (weights at the candidate) cancel: the sum of each parameter's terms, divided by the sum of the absolute values of those terms, is at rounding level. The oracle accepts a root at 1e-10 and a settled iteration at 1e-6. The normaliser must not contain the weights or the predictions, because those can be astronomically large at spurious points. Used as a test property for the engine (independent evaluation of Jᵀ·W·r).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the 12 Indometh subject–scheme pairs of `fit_indometh_inv_yhat` and `fit_indometh_inv_yhat2`; worked example F5 gives the cancellation measure at two of them (E-10).
- Sources: D; card T-018 (E-10).

## 3. Algorithm

### FIT-ALG-01 Default algorithm
The default minimiser is the Gauss-Newton method with the Levenberg and Hartley modification: a Gauss-Newton direction, a step-length reduction along it (Hartley), and a damping term added to the normal equations when the plain direction fails or the system is ill-conditioned (Levenberg). Another optimizer may be offered as an option, never as a silent replacement (`AGENTS.md` section 6). FIT-ALG-02 to FIT-ALG-05 specify what Caladrius does.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: S-23, S-24 and S-25 (the method specified below); S-24 (Levenberg: damped least squares), S-23 (Hartley: modified Gauss-Newton), S-25 (Marquardt: the scaled form).

### FIT-ALG-02 Gauss-Newton direction
At θ_k with weighted residual vector r = (√w_i·r_i) and weighted Jacobian J, the direction δ solves the normal equations (JᵀJ + λ·M)·δ = Jᵀr, with λ = 0 for the pure Gauss-Newton direction. M is the identity for Levenberg's original damping and diag(JᵀJ) for Marquardt's scale-invariant form; Caladrius uses diag(JᵀJ) (parameters such as V ≈ 10 and k ≈ 0.2 differ by orders of magnitude). Numerically the system is solved from J, with its columns scaled to equal length, by a QR (Householder) factorisation, not by forming JᵀJ explicitly, because JᵀJ squares the condition number. A pure Gauss-Newton step needs P ≤ N and a non-singular JᵀJ.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the iterates of worked example F1 (`oracle/expected/fit/gauss_newton_f1.csv`, WRSS and (V, k) for iterations 0 to 5 at 15 digits, compared at 1e-9 relative): the pure Gauss-Newton step with ν = 1 is accepted each time (E-10, E-11).
- Sources: S-24, S-25 (the damped normal equations); S-28 (Gauss-Newton, book level); D (setting the gradient of WRSS to zero and dropping the second-derivative term). Worked example F1.

### FIT-ALG-03 Step length (Hartley)
Given δ, try θ_k + ν·δ with ν = 1; while WRSS(θ_k + ν·δ) ≥ WRSS(θ_k) or the point is outside the bounds or the prediction is not finite, halve ν, down to ν_min = 2⁻¹⁰ (the default floor of R's `nls`, S-26). A step that decreases WRSS is accepted and the iteration counter advances.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: S-23 (the modification of Gauss-Newton by a step length); S-26 (step halving with a floor); design choice for the numbers.

### FIT-ALG-04 Damping (Levenberg)
If no ν ≥ ν_min decreases WRSS, or the factorisation reports a numerically singular system, repeat FIT-ALG-02 with λ > 0: start at λ = 1e-3, multiply by 10 after each failure up to 1e10; after a success divide λ by 10 (never below 0, returning to the pure Gauss-Newton direction). If λ = 1e10 still fails the run stops with status `no_decrease` (FIT-CNV-03).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: S-24, S-25 (increase the damping until the sum of squares falls); design choice for the factors.

### FIT-ALG-05 Determinism
The same input, options and initial estimates always give the same output and trace, on every platform (no randomisation, no threads that change summation order).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the determinism test of `oracle_fit.rs`: the same input gives identical output (E-10).
- Sources: golden rule 3 and 4 of `AGENTS.md` (the same command from UI, CLI and MCP); design choice.

## 4. Partial derivatives

### FIT-JAC-01 Partial derivatives: default and finite differences
Default (`derivatives = auto`, task T-040): the closed forms of FIT-JAC-02 when the model has them for the fitted parameters (every one-compartment model in the (V, k) parameterisation, every `pk2.*` model), otherwise forward differences with h = 1e-5; the method is chosen once, at the initial estimates, and the result says which one was used. A forward difference of the prediction with respect to θ_j is [f(θ + Δ_j·e_j) − f(θ)]/Δ_j with Δ_j = h·|θ_j| (a relative increment), and Δ_j = h when θ_j = 0. The increment `h` is a user option. The preset `reference_conventions` uses forward differences with h = 0.001 (D-04). It is an option of the engine, not a claim about another program.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: the default is a Caladrius choice (Q-014, option 2), not taken from another program: with it the 185 reference fits reach the exact minimum within 1e-4 (FIT-CNV-01); h = 1e-5 keeps the rounding error of a forward difference (about 1e-16/h) far below its truncation error (about h) and gave 0 of 490 estimates beyond 1e-4 with the tight stop (D-03). The preset uses a relative forward increment of 0.001 (D-04). Worked example F2 shows the size of the effect; the forward-difference standard errors are reproduced by the oracle test of FIT-JAC-03.

### FIT-JAC-02 Analytic derivatives (option)
The models of `specs/models.md` have closed-form derivatives. They may be used instead (`derivatives = analytic`), and the testkit uses them as an independent truth. For the IV bolus, ∂f/∂V = −f/V and ∂f/∂k = −t·f (in the (V, k) parameterisation). The engine has closed-form derivatives for every v1 model in the (V, k) parameterisation: `pk1.iv_bolus`, `pk1.oral_1` (with a series near ka = k), and since task T-030 `pk1.iv_infusion`, `pk1.oral_0`, `pk1.oral_1_lag` and `pk1.oral_0_lag` (the CL parameterisation has no closed forms in the engine: `model.rs` matches the (V, k) parameter sets only, so a fit in CL is refused with a readable error that says to use forward differences, `AnalyticDerivativesUnavailable`, for the IV bolus as well, which an earlier version of this sentence listed); `analytic` never falls back silently to forward differences (this sentence said the opposite until T-029, which found it by running the new fit oracles). The oracle's references use analytic derivatives. **Two compartments (T-033):** for every `pk2.*` id and each of its three parameter sets (clearance, micro, macro, `specs/models.md` MOD-2C-02) the closed-form derivatives come from the models crate (its `jacobian` with the method `Analytic`, MOD-2C-17 to 19 and 25), and the fit picks the columns of the parameters it fits (fixed parameters, such as `dur`, give no column); the same rule holds, `analytic` never falls back silently to forward differences. If the models crate cannot give the Jacobian, the model error is reported by the first prediction of the fit (as an initial-estimate error), and the message `AnalyticDerivativesUnavailable` for a `pk2.*` id names the parameter sets and no longer gives the (V, k) or CL advice of the one-compartment ids. Forward differences (FIT-JAC-01) work for the two-compartment ids through their predictions, and the standard errors of FIT-OUT-02 follow the Jacobian used, as FIT-JAC-03. Secondary parameters with delta-method standard errors exist for the one-compartment ids only (open item OF-08).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: all 90 reference fits are computed on the analytic Jacobian; the engine's analytic bolus and first-order derivatives reproduce them (E-10, E-11). For two compartments, the closed forms themselves are `confirmed by oracle` by the 64 derivative cases `model_pk2_deriv_*` of the model oracle (5 681 values, relative 1e-12; `specs/models.md` MOD-2C-19); there is no fit oracle for two compartments yet: the engine's unit tests check the fit's columns against central differences in the three sets and recover (cl, vc, q, vp, ka) from exact `pk2.oral_1` data by an analytic-derivative fit from given initial values (status of that part: engine tests only).
- Sources: D (calculus). Worked examples F1, F2.

### FIT-JAC-03 Which Jacobian the statistics use
The standard errors and the matrices of FIT-OUT-02 use the same Jacobian as the last iteration of the fit (closed forms by default when the model has them, FIT-JAC-01). With forward differences their size carries the relative error of the increment (about h/2 times the curvature); with h = 0.001 (the preset `reference_conventions`) this is of order 1e-3 relative on the standard errors (example F2).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the test "forward-difference SE of F2": with h = 0.001 the standard errors of the five-point example are 0.24814908 and 0.01281461 (E-10).
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6; D; H3.

## 5. Convergence and non-convergence

### FIT-CNV-01 Convergence criterion
Let WRSS_k be the value after accepted iteration k (WRSS_0 at the initial estimates). The fit has converged at iteration k when the relative decrease is small: (WRSS_{k−1} − WRSS_k) ≤ ε·WRSS_k, with ε = 1e-10 by default (task T-040, Q-014 option 2: a Caladrius choice, the value of the conformance runs FIT-CNV-04) and ε = 0.0001 in the preset `reference_conventions` (D-04). The result is θ_k. The engine adds two ways to end at a minimum that is as good as rounding allows: (a) after at least one accepted step, no trial step decreases WRSS and the decrease predicted by the Gauss-Newton model (‖J·δ‖²) is at most max(ε, h²)·WRSS with forward differences of increment h (ε·WRSS with analytic derivatives): below h² the predicted gain is within the error of the forward-difference Jacobian, so with ε = 1e-10 and h = 1e-3 a forward-difference fit may stop at a point whose predicted gain is up to 1e-6·WRSS, within the D-03 accuracy (T-030b); (b) an exact fit, WRSS ≤ 1e-26·Σ w·y². Neither is taken at the initial estimates: a start from which nothing can be improved is `no_decrease`, not `converged`. For predicted-value weights the test of FIT-CNV-05 replaces the plain one. Alternative criterion `relative_offset` (the one of R's `nls`: the size of the residual's component along the model's tangent plane relative to the part orthogonal to it, over the free parameters) is an option; it is scale-free and is the better test near a perfect fit.
- Status: `assumed`
- Oracle: the iteration count of F1: stop at k = 4 with ε = 0.0001 of the preset (unit test of the engine, and the trace of `gauss_newton_f1.csv`); the oracle itself runs with ε = 1e-10 (FIT-CNV-04). The default was measured on all 185 reference fits (`crates/caladrius-fit/tests/default_settings.rs`, a gate): with the default options every fit converges, in at most 22 iterations, and 0 of 490 estimates lie beyond 1e-4 of the reference (worst 7.4e-6, `fit_indometh_inv_y` subject 2, k); with the preset, 168 of 490 (worst 5.9e-3, D-03). The relative-offset criterion at 1e-5 also gives 0 of 490 (worst 7.8e-6); the relative decrease was kept because it is the rule of the conformance runs and of FIT-CNV-05. The value 0.0001 and the quantity it applies to are the open item OF-02.
- Sources: the default ε = 1e-10 is a Caladrius choice (Q-014); the preset's value: `AGENTS.md` section 6 ("convergence criterion 0.0001": which quantity it applies to is not stated, open item OF-02); S-26 (relative offset criterion, default 1e-5); D. Worked example F1 shows the stop after 4 iterations.

### FIT-CNV-02 Maximum number of iterations
At most 50 accepted iterations, by default and in the preset `reference_conventions` (with the default ε = 1e-10 the 185 reference fits need at most 22, FIT-CNV-01). Reaching 50 without meeting FIT-CNV-01 is not convergence (status `max_iterations`; the iteration-limit test of `oracle_fit.rs` checks it).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6; S-26 (default 50).

### FIT-CNV-05 Convergence with predicted-value weights
Because the weights are frozen inside an iteration (FIT-WGT-03), the decrease of WRSS measured with them understates how far the reweighting still has to go. With predicted-value weights the engine therefore estimates the linear rate of the iteration from the last two relative decreases, ρ = √(r_k / r_{k−1}), and declares convergence only when ρ < 1 and r_k ≤ ε·(1 − ρ)², that is when the expected remaining gain of the reweighting is below the criterion. This is stricter than the plain test (it reduces to it when ρ → 0), cannot fire at the first iteration, and never fires on a cycle (ρ ≥ 1) or on growing decreases. Before `converged` or `at_bound` is reported, the WRSS of the final estimates, computed with the weights of those same estimates (its own weights), is compared with that of the initial estimates; if it is larger, the status is `no_decrease` and the iterate with the lowest finite own-weight WRSS is returned, so a fit is never called converged when it ended worse than it began.
- Status: `assumed`
- Oracle: needed to reach the Indometh `inv_yhat2` subject 4 value at 1e-6 relative in WRSS: the plain test stopped at WRSS 2.0117000238 against the fixed point 2.0116979316 (1.04e-6 relative), because WRSS is not stationary at a reweighted fixed point (d ln WRSS / d ln V is 1 for `inv_yhat` and 2 for `inv_yhat2`), so an estimate error of 3e-7 appears as 1e-6 in WRSS (E-10, E-11). The orchestrator decided that the engine converges tighter rather than the tolerance being changed.
- Sources: design choice of card T-011a, reviewed (E-11).

### FIT-CNV-03 Status and reasons
Every fit ends with a status, never with a panic or a silent value:

| status | meaning | what is returned |
|---|---|---|
| `converged` | FIT-CNV-01 met | everything in section 7 |
| `max_iterations` | 50 iterations done, criterion not met | best iterate, trace, statistics flagged "not converged" |
| `no_decrease` | FIT-ALG-03/04 cannot decrease WRSS, or (predicted-value weights) the final own-weight WRSS is above that of the initial estimates (FIT-CNV-05) | best iterate, trace, statistics flagged |
| `singular` | JᵀJ has a zero eigenvalue at the best iterate: at least one parameter combination is not identifiable | best iterate; standard errors NC (`singular`); the combination (eigenvector of the zero eigenvalue, named by parameter) is reported |
| `non_finite` | the model gave a non-finite value at the start or at every trial | error before or at the first iteration, naming the parameters |
| `at_bound` | converged with at least one parameter on a bound | converged result, with the parameters on a bound listed (FIT-BND-02) |

Input that cannot be weighted, an unusable initial estimate or an unusable option is an error before the fit starts (FIT-ERR-01, FIT-ERR-04), not a status.

The text of every status says what to change (initial estimates, bounds, weights, model). The statistics of a result that did not converge are shown only with a visible "not converged" mark.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: golden rule 6 of `AGENTS.md` (non-convergence and singular matrix each give a readable error and a regression test); `AGENTS.md` section 7 (every error says what to fix).

### FIT-CNV-04 Conformance runs
Tests compare minima with a tight criterion (ε = 1e-10 on the relative decrease, 200 iterations) so that parameters are compared at the tolerances of `AGENTS.md` section 5 (1e-4 relative; WRSS 1e-6 relative); since task T-040 the user-facing default is the same ε = 1e-10 with 50 iterations (FIT-CNV-01), and ε = 1e-4 is the preset `reference_conventions`. With ε = 1e-4 the first example stops at parameters that are already within 1e-7 relative of the minimum (F1), but this is not guaranteed in general.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: every reference fit and `gauss_newton_f1.csv` use convergence 1e-10 and 200 iterations; the engine's results are compared at those settings (E-10).
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 5; D; H3.

## 6. Bounds and initial estimates

### FIT-BND-01 Bounds
Each parameter has an optional lower and upper bound (user supplied, default FIT-BND-03). The initial estimate must lie inside; otherwise an error names the parameter. A trial point outside the bounds is shortened (FIT-ALG-03); if the Gauss-Newton direction for a parameter that sits on a bound points outward, that parameter is held at the bound for the iteration and the direction is recomputed for the remaining free parameters (active-set projection).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6 ("parameter bounds generated by the software unless the user supplies them"); design choice for the projection.

### FIT-BND-02 Reporting a bound
A parameter held on a bound at the end gets the flag `at_bound`; it gets no standard error or interval; the other parameters' statistics are computed from the free-parameter block only (P and DF count the free parameters) and are conditional on the bound. A result with a parameter on a bound is never presented as an ordinary converged fit.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: design choice; `AGENTS.md` section 7 (poor fits must be flagged).

### FIT-BND-03 Default bounds
When the user supplies none, the engine generates bounds from the initial estimates: `v`, `cl`, `k`, `ka` and `dur` must stay above 1e-6 × their initial estimate, `tlag` ≥ 0, and there is no upper bound. An initial estimate outside its bounds is an error (`InitialOutsideBounds`). Parameters can also be held fixed (option `fixed`, for example `dur` of a zero-order model, which cannot be estimated from initial values alone); a fixed parameter given an initial estimate or a bound is refused.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6; `specs/models.md` MOD-GEN-04 (parameter domains); engine rule of card T-011b (E-11).

### FIT-INI-01 Initial estimates
User-supplied initial estimates are used as given. When absent, they are generated by the method of residuals (FIT-INI-02 to 04). Generated values are shown to the user and can be edited; the predicted curve updates live (`AGENTS.md` section 7). **Two-compartment ids (`pk2.*`, T-033):** no automatic initial estimates exist. A request for them is refused with a readable error that says the user must give every initial value (for the clearance set cl, vc, q, vp and, by id, ka, tlag, dur); nothing is guessed silently. The generation for two compartments (peeling with more phases) is open item OF-08.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` sections 6 and 7; S-28 (curve stripping, book level).

### FIT-INI-02 IV bolus
Fit the straight line ln y = a − k·t by ordinary least squares on all points with y > 0 (all points belong to the single phase of a one-compartment model; the last three or more if the user restricts it). Then k = −slope and V = D/e^a. Points with y ≤ 0 are skipped for this step only.
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: S-28 (log-linear regression for the one-compartment bolus, book level); `specs/nca.md` NCA-LZ-01 (the same regression); D. Worked example F4 uses the same machinery.

### FIT-INI-03 First-order absorption (curve stripping)
1. Terminal phase: choose the terminal points by the rule of `specs/nca.md` NCA-LZ-02 to NCA-LZ-05 (after the observed Tmax, y > 0, best adjusted R² within the tolerance, at least 3 points). Its line is ln y = a1 − k·t.
2. Residuals: for each point before the terminal window with a positive residual R(t) = e^(a1 − k·t) − y(t), the residuals follow A·e^(−ka·t) in a one-compartment model. Fit ln R = a2 − ka·t by ordinary least squares (at least 2 points; the points with R ≤ 0 are skipped).
3. Constants: A = e^(a1) for the model without lag. V/F = D·ka/((ka − k)·A).
4. Lag: for the model with lag, the two lines meet at tlag (D: A·e^(−k(t − tlag)) and A·e^(−ka(t − tlag)) are both equal to A at t = tlag), so tlag = (a1 − a2)/(k − ka), set to 0 if negative; and A = e^(a1 − k·tlag).
5. Flip-flop: if the residual slope ka comes out smaller than k, the roles are ambiguous (`specs/models.md` MOD-AB1-06); the default assigns the faster constant to ka and warns that the other solution exists.

If fewer than 3 terminal points or fewer than 2 positive residuals are available, no automatic estimate is produced: an error asks the user to enter estimates (FIT-ERR-03). The generated lag is kept below 0.9 times the first sampling time, so that no prediction is zero at the first sample. The engine reproduces example F4 (k 0.19968599, ka 1.0370213, V 9.9716735, tlag 0.0347); it resolves the flip-flop towards the faster constant silently, no warning channel exists yet (open item OF-11). Example F4 gives the numbers.
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: S-28 (method of residuals, book level); `specs/nca.md` NCA-LZ-02..05 (point selection); D. The lag-from-intersection step and the flip-flop warning are the reader's, tagged with the rule.

### FIT-INI-04 Infusion and zero-order input
Terminal phase after the end of the input (time T + tlag) as in FIT-INI-03 step 1, line ln y = a1 − k·t. The concentration at the end of the input is C_T = e^(a1 − k·(T + tlag)). With R0 = D/T: CL = R0·(1 − e^(−k·T))/C_T and V = CL/k (D: from MOD-IVI-01). If no point lies after the end of the input, only the rising phase is available and no automatic estimate is produced (FIT-ERR-03). The duration is not estimated from the data: it must be given as a fixed parameter (finite and > 0, otherwise the error names it).
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: `specs/models.md` MOD-IVI-01; S-28; D.

## 7. Outputs

The result of a fit contains the items below; each is NC with a reason when it cannot be computed (never NaN or infinity). Items 1 to 5 are the "final parameters" table.

### FIT-OUT-01 Final parameters
For each free parameter: the estimate, its standard error, CV%, univariate confidence interval (lower, upper), planar confidence interval (lower, upper). Fixed parameters are listed with their value and no statistics.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: estimate, SE, CV%, both intervals for every parameter of the 90 fits (E-10).
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4 (list of outputs of `caladrius-fit`); the definitions follow.

### FIT-OUT-02 Covariance, standard errors, CV%
Cov = S²·(JᵀJ)⁻¹ with J the weighted Jacobian at the solution and S² = WRSS/DF. SE_j = √Cov_jj. CV%_j = 100·SE_j/|θ̂_j| (NC if θ̂_j = 0). When DF = 0 or JᵀJ is singular, these are NC (`no_degrees_of_freedom`, `singular`).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: covariance, correlation, SE and CV% of the 90 fits; SE and S checked against `summary.nls` to 1e-6 in the oracle script (E-10).
- Sources: S-28 (asymptotic covariance of non-linear least squares, book level); D (linearisation of the model at the solution); S-26 (the usual `summary` of a non-linear fit is of this form). Worked example F2.

### FIT-OUT-03 Univariate confidence interval
θ̂_j ± t(1 − α/2; DF)·SE_j, with the Student quantile for DF degrees of freedom and α = 0.05 by default (level 95 %). Each interval is for one parameter taken alone.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: univariate limits of the 90 fits at level 95 % (`confidence_level` 0.95, Student quantile), compared at the derived tolerance for limits near zero explained in the test header (E-10).
- Sources: S-28 (book level); D. Worked example F2.

### FIT-OUT-04 Planar confidence interval
The joint (tangent-plane) confidence region of all parameters is the ellipsoid (θ − θ̂)ᵀ·(JᵀJ)·(θ − θ̂) ≤ P·S²·F(1 − α; P, DF). The planar interval of parameter j is the extent of this ellipsoid along axis j: θ̂_j ± √(P·F(1 − α; P, DF))·SE_j. It is wider than the univariate interval because it covers all parameters at once.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: planar limits of the 90 fits (factor √(P·F(0.95; P, DF))), E-10.
- Sources: S-28 (linear-approximation joint regions, book level); D (the extent of {x : xᵀAx ≤ c} along axis j is √(c·(A⁻¹)_jj)). Worked example F2.

### FIT-OUT-05 Correlation matrix, eigenvalues, condition number
Corr_jk = Cov_jk/(SE_j·SE_k) (symmetric, unit diagonal). Eigenvalues of the correlation matrix, in decreasing order. Condition number κ = largest/smallest eigenvalue of the correlation matrix (infinite when singular: NC). The condition number of the scaled Jacobian (columns of J normalised to unit length), √(λmax/λmin) of the scaled JᵀJ, is reported as `kappa_jacobian`. For two parameters the two sets of eigenvalues coincide (example F2).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: eigenvalues and condition number of the correlation matrix and the condition of the unit-column Jacobian, 90 fits (E-10).
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4 (eigenvalues and condition numbers listed without a definition); D.

### FIT-OUT-06 Secondary parameters
For a derived quantity g(θ) (CL = V·k, t½ = ln 2/k, AUC to infinity, Cmax, Tmax, …) the value g(θ̂) and the standard error √(∇gᵀ·Cov·∇g) (the delta method, gradient by differentiation of the model formulas or by the finite-difference increment), with CV% and the univariate interval as in FIT-OUT-02/03 using the same DF.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: CL, half-life and AUC to infinity with SE, CV% and interval by the delta method, 90 fits (E-10). The engine provides them through the model (`FitModel::secondary`); a custom model has none.
- Sources: S-28 (delta method, book level); `specs/models.md` MOD-SEC-02; D. Worked example F2 (CL and t½).

### FIT-OUT-07 Diagnostics
- weighted sum of squares of the observations: Σ w_i·y_i²;
- corrected weighted sum of squares: Σ w_i·(y_i − ȳ_w)² with ȳ_w = Σ w_i·y_i/Σ w_i (the weighted total variation around the weighted mean);
- weighted residual sum of squares WRSS (the objective), also called the residual SS;
- S = √(WRSS/DF), DF = N − P (NC for DF ≤ 0);
- correlation between observed and predicted: the weighted Pearson correlation of y and ŷ with weights w_i;
- AIC = N·ln(WRSS) + 2·P; SBC = N·ln(WRSS) + P·ln N.

The information criteria use this form (common in PK software and giving the same ranking as the likelihood form for the same data and weights); a constant N·(1 + ln(2π/N))-type term separates it from the likelihood form and from other programs' values (OF-07). NC if WRSS = 0 or the fit is exact (WRSS ≤ 1e-26·Σ w·y², where the logarithm is meaningless).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: Σ w·y², corrected sum, WRSS, S, observed/predicted correlation, AIC and SBC of the 90 fits; AIC and SBC are compared through the derived tolerance of the test header (E-10).
- Sources: S-27 (Akaike's criterion), S-29 (Schwarz's criterion), both as principles; the exact forms and the use of the weights are the reader's reading (OF-07); D. Worked example F2.

### FIT-OUT-08 Partial derivatives
A table with one row per observation and one column per parameter: the (unweighted) derivative ∂f(t_i)/∂θ_j at the solution, by the method of FIT-JAC-01 (or analytic). The weighted version √w_i·∂f/∂θ_j is available (empty when a final weight is not finite). The engine's table agrees with the analytic values of example F2 (its own unit test); the oracle has no partials file.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4; D. Worked example F2 prints the table.

### FIT-OUT-09 Predicted data and residuals
For each observation: time, observed, predicted, residual y − ŷ, weighted residual √w·(y − ŷ), weight. Residuals of excluded observations are shown with their exclusion reason and are not used in any sum.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4; design choice.

### FIT-OUT-10 Smooth predicted curve
`n_curve` = 1000 equally spaced times from the first to the last observation time (both included) with the predicted concentration, for plotting. The count is an option.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6 ("1000 predicted values for the curve").

### FIT-OUT-11 Minimization trace
One row per iteration, including iteration 0: iteration number, WRSS, the parameter vector, the step factor ν actually used, λ, the relative decrease of FIT-CNV-01, and the number of step halvings. The last row carries the status of FIT-CNV-03 and the reason for stopping. Never discarded, also for failed fits. With predicted-value weights the last row may show an iterate that was not returned (FIT-CNV-05): the trace is a record, the reported estimates are the best iterate.
- Status: `assumed`
- Oracle: the iterates of F1 (iteration, WRSS, V, k) at 15 digits (E-10); the step factor, λ and halving columns are not covered.
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4 (minimization trace); design choice. Worked example F1 prints a trace.

## 8. Quality flags and errors

### FIT-FLG-01 Flags
A poor fit is flagged, not hidden (`AGENTS.md` section 7, friction 4). The engine raises these flags, from the finished numbers, never feeding back into them:

| flag | raised when | default threshold |
|---|---|---|
| not converged | the status is not `converged` (or `at_bound`) | none |
| weak convergence | the last accepted step was damped (λ > 0) or shortened to ν ≤ 0.25, so that "converged" rests on a small decrease after a difficult step (for example a 3-point fit of a 3-parameter model); ordinary halvings are not flagged | none |
| at bound | a parameter ended on a bound | none |
| high CV | CV% of a parameter above the threshold | 50 |
| interval contains 0 | the univariate interval of a parameter that must be positive contains 0 | none |
| high correlation | \|correlation\| of two estimates above the threshold | 0.95 |
| ill-conditioned | condition number above the threshold | 1e6 |
| few degrees of freedom | DF below the threshold | 2 |

Thresholds are options, range-checked (CV% ≥ 0, correlation threshold in [0, 1], condition number ≥ 1, otherwise `InvalidOption`; a NaN threshold is refused). Each flag says what to check (fewer parameters, other initial estimates, a different weighting).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 7; design choice (thresholds, wording); implemented in card T-011b and reviewed (E-11).

### FIT-ERR-01 Input errors (before fitting)
Readable errors for: observation arrays of different length; fewer observations than free parameters (N < P); NaN or infinite time or concentration (a missing concentration is excluded with the reason `missing`); negative times for IV models; negative concentrations when the weights need positive values (FIT-WGT-02); an initial estimate outside its bounds; a dose ≤ 0; an unknown model or weighting id. In the engine, following the oracle test, a NaN concentration and a time before the dose are errors, not silent exclusions: the caller leaves such observations out (the data layer may offer the exclusion with the reasons `missing` and `before_dose`).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: golden rule 6 of `AGENTS.md`; `specs/nca.md` NCA-DAT-02, NCA-DAT-03; design choice.

### FIT-ERR-02 N = P and N − P small
With N = P (DF = 0) the fit can still run and reaches WRSS ≈ 0 when it converges; S, standard errors, intervals and AIC are NC (`no_degrees_of_freedom`) and the result is flagged "exact fit, no precision information". The same holds for an exact fit with DF > 0 (WRSS ≤ 1e-26·Σ w·y²): AIC and SBC are NC there, since ln WRSS is meaningless. With DF ≥ 1 everything is computed, and DF < 2 is flagged.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the N = P test of `oracle_fit.rs` (the statistics are absent, not NaN), E-10.
- Sources: D (S² = WRSS/DF is undefined for DF = 0); design choice.

### FIT-ERR-03 Initial estimates cannot be generated
Error text: the terminal phase has fewer than 3 usable points, or there are no earlier points to form residuals, or the residual line rises; the user is asked for estimates, with the data reduced to what was usable shown beside it.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: golden rule 6 and `AGENTS.md` section 7; design choice.

### FIT-ERR-04 Options are range-checked
Options that could exhaust memory or make the run meaningless are refused before anything is allocated, with `InvalidOption` naming the option: `n_curve` must be 0 or between 2 and 1 000 000, `convergence` between 0 and 0.1, `max_iterations` at most 100 000, `increment` in (0, 0.1], the flag thresholds as in FIT-FLG-01. Unknown option names are errors too (a misspelled option never runs silently with the default).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: reviewer findings on card T-011a (an `n_curve` of 2⁴⁰ aborted the process), golden rule 6; design choice (E-11).

## 10. Why the defaults are credible: a note on convergence

(No rule.) Near a minimum with small residuals the Gauss-Newton iteration converges quadratically, so when the relative decrease of WRSS has fallen to 1e-4 the parameters are usually already much closer to the minimum than 1e-4 (example F1: 1e-7). With large residuals the convergence is linear and the same stopping rule can stop far from the minimum; that is why conformance runs use a tight criterion (FIT-CNV-04) and why the trace is always returned.

### Tolerance note: why 1e-4 for fit parameters and 1e-6 for NCA (task T-029)

(No rule; the contract is `AGENTS.md` section 5.) NCA is closed arithmetic on the data: trapezoids, a regression on a chosen window, a few divisions. PKNCA and Caladrius run the same finite sequence of operations, so they differ by rounding and by nothing else, and 1e-6 relative leaves room for the digits PKNCA's own rounding drops. A fit is a search. Three things make two correct fits differ by more than rounding, which is why the contract allows 1e-4 relative on parameters and keeps 1e-6 for the weighted sum of squares:

1. **Stopping rule.** `nlsLM` stops at `ftol = ptol = 1e-15`; the engine stops when the relative decrease of WRSS is below ε (1e-10 in the tests). Near a minimum WRSS is flat, so a parameter error δ costs only about δ² in WRSS, and a rule on the decrease stops with δ of order √ε: 1e-5 for ε = 1e-10 (the user default since T-040; with ε = 1e-4 of the preset `reference_conventions` the engine only guarantees what example F1 shows, FIT-CNV-04).
2. **Partial derivatives.** With forward differences (h = 0.001 in the preset `reference_conventions`, FIT-JAC-01) the iteration stops where the approximated gradient vanishes, not the true one. The oracle uses analytic derivatives, and so do the tests and, since T-040, the default fit when the model has closed forms. With h = 0.001 the estimates differ from the exact minimum by up to 7e-4 on Indometh (`specs/differences.md` D-03).
3. **Reweighted schemes.** For `inv_yhat` and `inv_yhat2` the target is a fixed point, not a minimum, and WRSS is not flat there (OF-10).

**Measured, not assumed.** `cargo test -p caladrius-fit --test oracle_fit_tight -- --ignored --nocapture` compares the engine (analytic derivatives, ε = 1e-10) with the reference on the 95 fits of the older models and prints the worst gap per quantity: **7.4e-6** on an estimate (`fit_indometh_inv_y`, subject 2, k), **9.2e-6** on a standard error (`fit_theoph_inv_y`, subject 9, ka), **3.8e-7** on WRSS (`fit_indometh_inv_yhat2`, subject 4). The margins are therefore about 14 times on estimates (1e-4 / 7.35e-6), 11 times on standard errors (1e-4 / 9.18e-6) and only 2.6 times on WRSS (1e-6 / 3.82e-7), the last on a reweighted scheme where WRSS is not flat (OF-10). Taken together, the tolerances are a margin of caution against a different optimizer, not a cushion that was needed to make tests pass; no test was ever relaxed to get there (the one WRSS case that was too coarse was fixed by converging tighter, FIT-CNV-05, OF-10).

**One small case at 1e-6.** The five-point example of section 11 (F1 to F2: 5 observations, 2 parameters, uniform weights; JᵀJ and Jᵀr are 2 × 2) is small enough to be worked on paper, but nobody did: what exists is a second scripted recurrence. Precisely: (1) the Gauss-Newton iterates are reproduced to 15 digits by `gauss_newton_f1.csv`, written by the same R oracle script as the references, and its iteration 5 and the `nlsLM` estimates agree to 1e-8, as do the engine's; (2) the F2 digits printed below come from the reader's throwaway script, and the testkit checks the oracle against them to 1e-7; (3) the other statistics (standard errors, limits, eigenvalues, AIC, secondary parameters) have no independent value behind them: there the engine and R agree to 9.8e-9 on the estimates and 1.3e-8 on the standard errors, and the test `oracle_fit_tight.rs` holds every parameter, statistic, interval limit and secondary parameter of that fit to **1e-6** (`Tolerance::FIT_PARAMETERS_SMALL_CASE`, stricter than the contract and never used to replace it). This shows that 1e-4 is caution, not that the fit statistics were verified by hand.

### 13.1 Rules

### FIT-ML-01 Scope and selection
The maximum-likelihood objective is selected by the option `objective = ml` (default `wls`: sections 2 to 8, unchanged). With `ml` the user must name an `error_model` (FIT-ML-02); there is no silent default, because the choice changes the answer. The weighting schemes of FIT-WGT-01 do not apply: the weights are implied by the error model, so `objective = ml` together with a `weighting` other than the default `uniform` is refused with `InvalidOption` ("the maximum-likelihood fit derives its weights from the error model; remove the weighting option"). `uniform` is accepted because it is what the option holds when nobody set it; it is used only to produce the starting point (FIT-ML-10). The fit reads the observations of one profile as in FIT-DAT-01: missing and BLQ rows are left out and counted. A table with several subjects needs the subject to be named, as for the least-squares fit (`specs/data.md` DAT-USE-02). Everything else of sections 3 to 8 that does not mention WRSS, weights or S² is unchanged: the models, the derivatives (FIT-JAC-01 to 03), the bounds on θ (FIT-BND-01, 03), the initial estimates of θ (FIT-INI-01 to 04), the quality flags (FIT-FLG-01) and the input errors (FIT-ERR-01).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: design choice of this card; `AGENTS.md` section 6 (the least-squares options stay as they are).

### FIT-ML-02 Error models
The observation is y_i = ŷ_i + σ_i·ε_i with ε_i independent standard normal, and σ_i a function of the prediction and of the error parameters.

| id | σ_i | ξ | Q | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `additive` | a | (a) | 1 | constant standard deviation |
| `proportional` | b·\|ŷ_i\| | (b) | 1 | constant coefficient of variation |
| `combined` | √(a² + b²·ŷ_i²) | (a, b) | 2 | the variances of an additive and a proportional part add |
| `combined_sd` | a + b·\|ŷ_i\| | (a, b) | 2 | the standard deviations add |

a ≥ 0 and b ≥ 0 are the parameters the user sees. They are not always the quantities that the iteration moves (FIT-ML-05). The prediction enters through its absolute value: a negative prediction is legal for the proportional and combined models, and σ_i = 0 is not (FIT-ML-14). The same families exist in the open tools. `nlme` has a power variance function (power fixed at 1 it is the proportional model); a constant-plus-power function whose standard deviation is a sum of a constant and a power of the absolute covariate (with the power fixed at 1 it is the `combined_sd` form: a = σ·t1 and b = σ for its constant t1 and scale σ); and a constant-plus-proportional function whose variance is a sum of a constant and a squared proportional part (the `combined` form; its help page recommends fixing the scale at 1, and then its two parameters are a and b). `nlmixr2` lists both combined forms under two names (the variance sum as `combined2`, the standard-deviation sum as `combined1`; read through a documentation summary only).
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: S-39 (help pages of `varPower`, `varConstPower`, `varConstProp`, `varComb`, `varClasses`, read in full: the three variance functions and the recommendation about the scale); S-43 (names of the two combined forms in `nlmixr2`, summary only); D (the table).

### FIT-ML-03 The two combined forms, the default, and what is offered
Both forms are offered: `combined` (variance sum, σ² = a² + b²·ŷ²) and `combined_sd` (standard-deviation sum, σ = a + b·|ŷ|). **The default combined form is the variance sum**, and the interface names it `combined`. Reasons: (1) the two parts are read as independent additive and proportional noises, whose variances add, which is the usual statistical model for independent error sources; (2) σ² is smooth in (a, b) and in ŷ everywhere, including at ŷ = 0, and a and b enter squared, so no sign condition on a or b is needed and the boundary value 0 has a regular derivative in the variable the iteration uses (FIT-ML-05); (3) it is the first entry of the `nlmixr2` list of combined models (summary) and `nlme` offers it with the scale fixed. The standard-deviation sum is kept because the other family of tools uses it as its first combined form and the user must be able to reproduce their fits. The two forms give different estimates of (a, b) and different −2LL on the same data (example ML4: 0.2350 against 1.0929), so the form is part of the model and is recorded in the result.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: S-39 (variance-sum and standard-deviation-sum functions of `nlme`); S-43 (`nlmixr2` names and order, summary only); D; the reasons are the reader's.

### FIT-ML-04 The objective, and extended least squares
For one profile with N observations,

−2LL(θ, ξ) = Σ_i [ ln(2π·σ_i²) + (y_i − ŷ_i)²/σ_i² ],  σ_i = g(ŷ_i(θ); ξ).

The constant N·ln(2π) is kept, so the value is the true −2 log-likelihood of the Gaussian model (the `logLik` of a `gnls` fit, times −2, S-39). Extended least squares (Beal and Sheiner, 1988) minimises Σ [ln σ_i² + r_i²/σ_i²], the same function without the constant: **the minimiser is the same, the value differs by N·ln(2π)**. Two consequences. (1) A value of −2LL has no meaning alone: a change of the unit of y (y, ŷ and σ all multiplied by c) adds 2N·ln c, so −2LL can be negative or zero, and only differences between fits of the same observations are meaningful. (2) The variance estimate of the maximum-likelihood fit divides by N, not by DF = N − P, so â² = RSS/N and not S²; it is the maximum-likelihood estimate, biased downward, not the S² of FIT-OUT-07. Least squares with fixed weights w_i is the case σ_i² = s²/w_i with the one scale s² eliminated: −2LL is then N·(1 + ln(2π·WRSS/N)) − Σ ln w_i (D), which is how the WRSS forms of FIT-OUT-07 relate to the likelihood (FIT-ML-16).
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: S-47 (Beal and Sheiner 1988 for extended least squares, through a search summary; paper not read); S-39 (`logLik.gnls`: the log-likelihood of the model at the estimates; only the non-REML form exists); D (the identities); H24.

### FIT-ML-05 What the iteration moves: error parameters on a variance scale, bounded at 0
θ stays on its natural scale with its own bounds (FIT-BND-01, 03). The error parameters are moved as follows, always on a natural, not a logarithmic, scale:

| error model | moved variables | lower bound | reported as |
|---|---|---|---|
| `additive` | A = a² | 0 | a = √A |
| `proportional` | B = b² | 0 | b = √B |
| `combined` | A = a², B = b² | 0 and 0 | a = √A, b = √B |
| `combined_sd` | a, b themselves | 0 and 0 | a, b |

The bound is held by the active-set rule of FIT-BND-01: a variable on its bound whose step would be negative is held for that iteration, the step is recomputed for the others, and every variable is tested again at every iteration. Why not a log scale (the first idea): when a component such as the additive part of a combined model tends to 0, ln a tends to −∞ and the information about it, in the log scale, tends to 0; the iteration then stalls far from the boundary and its stopping test fires early (measured in the prototype: a stop at a = 2e-4 with −2LL too high by 0.026, where the true optimum is a = 0). A floor such as 1e-6 times the starting value would only move the problem. In the variance scale the derivative of ln σ with respect to A (variance sum) is 1/(2σ²), finite at A = 0, so the boundary solution a = 0 is reached exactly, with no floor constant. A fit that ends with a component on its bound is a valid converged fit with status `at_bound` (FIT-ML-15). For `additive` and `proportional` (Q = 1) the bound is never active at a sound optimum: reaching it is the exact-fit case (FIT-ML-15).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: design choice, supported by the prototype runs of H28 (four parameterisations tried; the log scale with a floor failed on the combined fits of examples ML3 and ML5, the variance scale did not); FIT-BND-01 for the projection.

### FIT-ML-06 What is iterated: Fisher scoring written as a Gauss-Newton step on a stacked residual vector
−2LL is not a sum of squares, but its iteration can use the same code as the least-squares fit, with a stacked system of 2N rows. Put ζ = (θ, ξ), n = P + Q, and for each observation

- u_ij = (∂ŷ_i/∂ζ_j)/σ_i (only the θ columns are non-zero),
- l_ij = ∂ ln σ_i/∂ζ_j (for a θ column this goes through the prediction: (∂ ln σ_i/∂ŷ_i)·∂ŷ_i/∂θ_j),
- z_i = r_i/σ_i.

Half the negative gradient of −2LL is g_j = Σ_i [ z_i·u_ij + (z_i² − 1)·l_ij ] (D: differentiate ln σ_i² and r_i²/σ_i², writing the derivative of σ_i through ln σ_i). The expected information of the log-likelihood (the expected outer product of the per-observation score, using E z² = 1 and E z⁴ = 3) is I = Σ_i [ u_i·u_iᵀ + 2·l_i·l_iᵀ ]. Both come from one stacked system:

- residual vector R of length 2N: the first block is z_i, the second block (z_i² − 1)/√2;
- matrix 𝒥 of 2N × n: row i is u_i (zeros in the ξ columns), row N + i is √2·l_i.

Then g = 𝒥ᵀR and I = 𝒥ᵀ𝒥, and the Gauss-Newton step δ solving (𝒥ᵀ𝒥)·δ = 𝒥ᵀR is the Fisher-scoring step δ = I⁻¹g. It is a quasi-Newton step on −2LL whose Hessian approximation is 2I (the second derivatives of the model are dropped, as FIT-ALG-02 does, and the part of the Hessian that depends on the residuals is replaced by its expected value). All of FIT-ALG-02 applies to this system unchanged: columns scaled to equal length, QR (Householder) on the scaled stacked matrix, the damped form (𝒥ᵀ𝒥 + λ·diag(𝒥ᵀ𝒥)) of FIT-ALG-04, no explicit 𝒥ᵀ𝒥. The derivatives of ln σ_i are closed forms:

| error model | ∂ ln σ/∂ŷ | ∂ ln σ/∂(moved variable) |
|---|---|---|
| `additive` | 0 | 1/(2A) |
| `proportional` | 1/ŷ | 1/(2B) |
| `combined` | B·ŷ/σ² | 1/(2σ²) for A, ŷ²/(2σ²) for B |
| `combined_sd` | b·sgn(ŷ)/σ | 1/σ for a, \|ŷ\|/σ for b |

With the additive model the θ rows and the ξ row do not interact (𝒥 has no cross terms) and one step lands on the exact solution (FIT-ML-11). The score and the information use the Jacobian of the model of FIT-JAC-01 (closed forms when available).
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: D (derivation above; the verification is that a scripted version of the stacked step reproduces, to 1e-8 on −2LL, the minimum found by `stats::optim` on the −2LL written directly, on the F data, the seed-1 profile and six Indometh subjects, H28); S-24, S-25 (the damped normal equations that the stacked system reuses); S-47 (the objective being minimised).

### FIT-ML-07 Step control
The iteration is that of FIT-ALG-02 to FIT-ALG-04 with −2LL in the place of WRSS. From the current ζ, compute δ (FIT-ML-06) with the active set of FIT-ML-05 and FIT-BND-01. Try ζ + ν·δ with ν = 1 and halve ν down to 2⁻¹⁰ while the trial does not lower −2LL. A trial is rejected, like a step that does not decrease −2LL, if a prediction is not finite, if any σ_i is not finite or not strictly positive, if a bound of θ is violated; a moved error variable that would become negative is first projected to 0 (FIT-ML-05). If no ν works, add Levenberg damping (λ from 1e-3, ×10 on failure up to 1e10, ÷10 after a success, FIT-ALG-04); if λ = 1e10 fails, stop with `no_decrease`. Each accepted trial counts one iteration. Determinism (FIT-ALG-05) holds.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: S-23, S-24, S-25, S-26 (as FIT-ALG-03 and 04); design choice for carrying them over to −2LL.

### FIT-ML-08 Convergence, the iteration limit, and the status
**Test.** At the current iterate, before the step, compute the Newton decrement Δ = gᵀ·I⁻¹·g over the free variables, with the undamped direction (λ = 0, even if the last step needed damping). Δ is the decrease of −2LL that the quadratic model predicts for a full step. The fit has converged when Δ ≤ ε, with ε = 1e-10 by default and **absolute**. Why absolute, and why Δ: the relative decrease of FIT-CNV-01 divides by the objective, and −2LL can be negative or zero (FIT-ML-04), so a relative rule is meaningless; Δ does not depend on the units of y or on the scale of the moved variables, so one ε serves every error model. At convergence the result is ζ_k.

**Limit.** For `objective = ml` the default iteration limit is **500** accepted iterations, not 50 (`max_iterations` is the same option, range-checked by FIT-ERR-04). Reason (measured, H28): Fisher scoring converges quickly only when the expected and the observed information are close, which is the case for a good fit of a well-identified model; on weakly identified combined models, and on the proportional model fitted to a profile that the model describes badly, the rate is linear and slow. On Indometh with the one-compartment model (a poor description, FIT-WGT-03), over the six subjects the proportional model took 17 to 297 iterations, the combined model 17 to 138 and the standard-deviation sum 13 to 227 (the additive model 0); with a limit of 50, 9 of the 24 fits (6 subjects × 4 models) stopped on the limit, at a −2LL within 0.1 of the optimum. With a limit of 500 all 24 converged, to a −2LL within 1.3e-9 of the one found by `optim` on the same data. On the well-fitting examples of section 13.2 the counts are 0 to 9 (additive and proportional: 0, 3 and 9) and 11 to 23 (the combined forms). The limit is therefore a guard, not a bound on the work of a good fit. These counts are the prototype's; the engine's, measured in T-149, are in OF-19 (largest 229) and, like the prototype's, are informational: no test pins an iteration count.

**Endgame: the Newton polish** (decided on T-149, fix round, engine commit eae738c, 2026-11-07; accepted by the orchestrator as the endgame brought forward from OF-19). After the test Δ ≤ ε has fired, the engine continues with Newton steps on the free variables, using the observed information: central differences of the analytic score, one column per free variable. The acceptance rule is exactly this: a Newton step is taken if and only if, at the trial point, (1) −2LL is not greater than at the current point, and (2) the scaled score Δ = gᵀ·I⁻¹·g strictly decreases. A trial whose set of free variables differs from the current one (a variable projected onto a bound) is rejected, because the two Δ would be measured on different parameters (T-149 review, A2, fixed). The polish stops when a step is rejected, after at most 12 steps, when Δ is at most 1e-26, or when the trial point cannot be evaluated. Each accepted step is its own trace row, marked `polish` (ν = 1, λ = 0, no halving), and the returned values are the last row's. The polish rows do not count as iterations in the counts of OF-19. Least-squares traces carry no such mark. The fixed point is that of the scoring iteration: only the distance to it changes. **Why it exists** (the T-149 measure, made once with the polish switched off and not committed): without it `oracle_fit_ml` passes 454 of 477 tests. The linear rate of Fisher scoring leaves the estimates about 1e-5 standard errors from the optimum when Δ ≤ 1e-10 fires. Thirteen tests of the least-squares forms miss the relative tolerance 1e-6 on WRSS (the worst, 3.80e-5, is `ml_indometh_combined` subject 6); `ml_indometh_combined_sd` subject 6 misses on estimates, standard errors and correlations at about 4.3e-4 and on IWRES; `ml_theoph_t0_combined_sd` subject 1 misses at about 1.6e-4; three IWRES tests miss the absolute tolerance 1e-5 (the worst, 6.89e-5). It matters most for combined fits whose `error_b` is tiny. With the polish, there are 165 polish rows over 117 fits (115 fits have at least one, none more than 2), and the worst error per tolerance at eae738c is 4.85e-9 relative on parameters, 6.04e-14 absolute on −2LL and 9.09e-8 absolute on IWRES. `AGENTS.md` section 5 asks for tighter convergence, not wider tolerances, which is the orchestrator's reason for accepting it. A faster approach, which would make the polish unnecessary, is open item OF-19.

**Status.** The statuses of FIT-CNV-03 apply: `converged` (Δ ≤ ε), `max_iterations` (the limit was reached; the statistics are flagged "not converged"; this is not convergence even when −2LL is within rounding of the optimum), `no_decrease`, `singular` (𝒥ᵀ𝒥 has a zero eigenvalue at the best iterate), `non_finite`, `at_bound` (converged with a bound active, FIT-ML-15), and `exact_fit` (FIT-ML-15). The text of `max_iterations` for this objective says that the fit is slow rather than lost, and suggests raising the limit, the other combined form, a simpler error model or better initial estimates.
- Status: `assumed`
- Tests: the polish is exercised by `oracle_fit_ml` (T-148 oracle, 488 of 488 on the merged branch) and by engine unit tests (the trace row, bitwise equality of the result and the last row, the cut by `max_iterations`, the rejection of a trial with another free set).
- Sources: design choice of ε and of the limit; T-149 thread (the polish, 2026-11-07); D (Δ as the predicted gain, FIT-ML-06); measurements H28 (the iteration counts above, from the reader's prototype, not an oracle); S-26 (an iteration limit with a distinct non-convergence status).

### FIT-ML-09 Why the iteration decreases −2LL, where it converges, and where it can fail
(a) **Descent.** If I is positive definite, δ = I⁻¹g is a descent direction for −2LL: the derivative of −2LL along δ is −2gᵀδ = −2Δ < 0 (D). A step short enough therefore lowers −2LL, the halving of FIT-ML-07 finds it, and −2LL decreases at every accepted iteration. (b) **Limit points.** A decreasing sequence of −2LL that stays in a region where −2LL is bounded below converges to a stationary point (g = 0, or a point on a bound with a zero projected gradient), which is what Δ ≤ ε tests. (c) **Where −2LL is not bounded below.** If some ζ makes all residuals vanish (an exact fit) and the error parameters can shrink with them, −2LL → −∞ and there is no maximum likelihood: FIT-ML-15 refuses this case. An observation of 0 that the model predicts as 0 for every parameter value is another way to reach −∞ under a combined error model: FIT-ML-13c. (d) **Local minima.** As for least squares, the minimum is local (FIT-OBJ-01); the start of FIT-ML-10 is a defence, not a guarantee. (e) **Speed.** The convergence is fast only when the expected and observed information agree near the optimum; otherwise it is linear (FIT-ML-08). (f) **Boundary.** A component that would become negative is held at 0 by the active set; the iteration then converges to the constrained optimum.
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: D (descent direction; convergence of a descent method with a step-length rule to a stationary point); S-23, S-24, S-25 (the modification of Gauss-Newton by step length and damping); H28 (the observations on speed).

### FIT-ML-10 Starting values, and initial estimates of the error parameters
**θ.** Two candidate starts are produced and the one with the lower −2LL is used (a tie goes to the first): (1) the least-squares estimate with uniform weights, obtained with the machinery of sections 3 to 8 from the usual initial estimates of θ (FIT-INI-01 to 04, or the user's); (2) when every observation is positive, the estimate with weights 1/y_i² (`inv_y2`, whose weights do not depend on θ). For `additive` only the first is produced, since it is already the solution (FIT-ML-11). **ξ.** For each candidate θ compute ŷ, r = y − ŷ and the robust scale m_i² = (ŷ_i² + y_i²)/2, then A₀ = Σ r_i²/N and B₀ = mean(r_i²/m_i²), and take

| error model | ξ₀ (in the moved variables) |
|---|---|
| `additive` | A₀ |
| `proportional` | B₀ |
| `combined` | (A₀/2, B₀/2) |
| `combined_sd` | (√A₀/2, √B₀/2) |

If the user supplies `error_init` (a positive value per error parameter, in the user's units a, b), it replaces ξ₀, and only the θ candidates are compared at it; a value ≤ 0 is `InvalidOption`. Why m_i and two candidates (measured, H28): the obvious choice B₀ = mean(r²/ŷ²) explodes when the least-squares fit predicts a value close to 0 where the observation is not, and the uniform fit can be a poor start for a proportional error. On Indometh subject 1 the uniform-fit start with b₀ from ŷ gives b₀ ≈ 383 and −2LL ≈ 2e6; with the rule above the chosen start (the `inv_y2` candidate) has −2LL = −2.46 and the fit converges (87 iterations, example ML5).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: design choice; H28 (the failure of the plain start and of the log scale, and the run with the start above); D (moment estimates of a variance from squared residuals); FIT-INI-01 to 04 for θ.

### FIT-ML-11 The additive model: an exact solution that is a hand check
For `additive`, θ̂_ML is the unweighted least-squares estimate θ̂_OLS and â² = RSS/N (D: for fixed A, −2LL = N·ln(2πA) + RSS(θ)/A, minimised in θ by minimising RSS(θ), then in A at A = RSS/N). Hence

−2LL = N·(1 + ln(2π·RSS/N)),  AIC_ML = that + 2·(P + 1),  BIC_ML = that + (P + 1)·ln N,

and the standard errors of θ̂ are the least-squares ones multiplied by √(DF/N) (D: I_θθ = JᵀJ/â², so Cov_θ = (RSS/N)·(JᵀJ)⁻¹, against S²·(JᵀJ)⁻¹ = (RSS/DF)·(JᵀJ)⁻¹). The engine must reach this fit in 0 iterations after the uniform least-squares fit (Δ = 0 at the start, since g_θ = 0 at the OLS point and A₀ = RSS/N), and its θ̂ equals that of the least-squares fit of sections 2 to 8 to rounding. Example ML1.

### FIT-ML-12 The proportional model is not `inv_yhat2` reweighting (OF-04)
Eliminating B (B̂ = mean of (r_i/ŷ_i)², so z_i = r_i/(b̂·|ŷ_i|)), the proportional model minimises

N·ln Σ_i (r_i/ŷ_i)² + 2·Σ_i ln|ŷ_i|  (plus the constant N·(1 + ln(2π/N)), D).

The stationarity condition in θ_j is Σ_i [ r_i/(b²·ŷ_i²) + (z_i² − 1)/ŷ_i ]·∂ŷ_i/∂θ_j = 0 (from g_j of FIT-ML-06). The fixed point of the `inv_yhat2` reweighting (FIT-WGT-03) solves Σ_i r_i/ŷ_i²·∂ŷ_i/∂θ_j = 0. They differ by the term Σ_i (z_i² − 1)/ŷ_i·∂ŷ_i/∂θ_j, which comes from the dependence of σ_i on θ and which the reweighting treats as a constant. It is not zero in general, so the two estimates differ, and the difference grows with the size of b (ML2: b = 0.033, relative differences 1.1e-4 in V and 1.8e-4 in k, −2LL apart by 4.5e-5; ML5: b = 0.44, 0.7 % and 0.6 %). The two also differ in the scale they report: the reweighting reports S² = WRSS/DF with DF = N − P, the ML fit b̂² = Σ(r/ŷ)²/N. The engine's ML proportional fit must therefore not be compared with the oracle's `inv_yhat2` fits, and the ML oracle needs its own references (OF-12). This answers the question OF-04 raised for the proportional case, as a difference; it does not turn the reweighting into a minimiser.
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: D (the two stationarity conditions); H25 (R: the profile function above evaluated and minimised with `optim`, and the `inv_yhat2` fixed point computed by iteration on the same data); FIT-WGT-03.

### FIT-ML-13 Properties at the optimum (a) and (b); the collapse is FIT-ML-13d, the unbounded likelihood FIT-ML-13c
(a) **Σ z_i² = N** at any converged fit with at least one error parameter above 0, for the four models. Reason (D): each σ_i is homogeneous of degree 1 in the error parameters, so multiplying them all by c and setting the derivative of −2LL at c = 1 to 0 gives Σ(2 − 2z_i²) = 0. It holds with one component of a combined model on its bound. It is a cheap self-check for any implementation. (b) **Σ z_i·sgn(ŷ_i) = 0 for the proportional model when V is a pure scale of ŷ** (every one-compartment IV model in V): from g_V = 0 and (a). With positive predictions the mean of the IWRES is 0 and their sample standard deviation with divisor N − 1 is exactly √(N/(N − 1)) (examples ML2: 1.118034 for N = 5).

### FIT-ML-13c An observation of 0 that the model predicts as 0 for every parameter value: the likelihood is unbounded
(Decisions of T-148, Q2, 2026-11-07, and of the orchestrator on the T-149 review, A1, and the T-149 thread, 0e84e68, 2026-11-07.)

**The problem.** Under `combined` and `combined_sd` an observation y_i = 0 whose prediction is ŷ_i = 0 for every parameter value has residual 0 and standard deviation σ_i = a (both forms give σ = a at ŷ = 0). Its term in −2LL is ln(2π·a²), which tends to −∞ as a tends to 0, while the other rows stay bounded. There is no maximum likelihood, and an iteration that is not stopped follows a to 0 and reports a −2LL that means nothing (FIT-ML-09c). The run is therefore refused before fitting, with the code `unbounded_likelihood`, and the error names the rows (their positions and times) and offers two remedies: exclude them, or use `additive`. Under `additive` the term is bounded (σ = a is shared by all rows), and the same data fit.

**The rows, by route.**
1. **At the dose time, t = 0, on every route that is not a bolus** (first-order oral, zero-order, infusion, with or without a lag): the prediction is 0 for every θ. The rule applies under `combined` and `combined_sd`. Under `proportional` the same row has σ = 0 and is `NonPositiveSigma` (FIT-ML-14b), not unbounded. Example: a Theoph subject whose sample at t = 0 is exactly 0 (nine of the twelve, ML6e), `pk1.oral_1`, `combined`: refused with the words "time 0", "exclude" and "additive"; with `additive` the fit runs.
2. **On a route with a lag, at a time t0 > 0.** On `pk1.oral_1_lag` and `pk1.oral_0_lag` (and on every other route with a lag) the prediction is exactly 0 for every t ≤ tlag, and tlag has no upper bound by default (FIT-BND-03). The rule: under `combined`, `combined_sd` **or `proportional`**, a row with y = 0 at a time t0 > 0 is refused before fitting when **the domain of tlag meets [t0, t_nz)**. Here t_nz is the time of the first nonzero observation (+∞ if there is none), and the domain of tlag is [lower, upper], which is [0, ∞) when the user sets no bound; a fixed tlag = c counts as [c, c]. The domain meets [t0, t_nz) when upper ≥ t0 and lower < t_nz. Since t0 < t_nz, every observation up to t0 is also 0 (a leading zero). The error names every such row.

**Why the lag rule is what it is** (D, and the oracle's 256-bit evidence). With tlag at t0 the zero row, and the zeros before it, are predicted exactly. For `combined` and `combined_sd` the term of each is ln(2π·a²) and −2LL → −∞ as a → 0. For `proportional` the fit puts tlag just below t0: the residual and σ are both of the order of t0 − tlag, z² stays 1/b², and ln σ² → −∞. A zero that follows a nonzero observation does not do this: with tlag at its time the earlier nonzero row is predicted 0 with a residual y ≠ 0, and −2LL goes to +∞ instead. The same holds when the domain lies at or above t_nz (a fixed tlag, or a lower bound, at or above t_nz). An upper bound on tlag below t0 keeps the prediction of the zero row away from 0, and the limit is finite. The evidence is `oracle/scripts/ml_lag_unbounded.R`, which computes −2LL at 256 bits along these paths on a one-subject profile (`pk1.oral_1_lag`, D = 100, times 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24, the first sample set to exactly 0, b = 0.1) and writes `oracle/expected/fit/ml/ml6f_lag_paths.json` (144 records, j = 1 to 50). Values read there for `pk1.oral_1_lag`: with tlag = 0.5 and a = 10^−j, `combined` falls from −1.8766658 (j = 1) to −221.45336 (j = 50) and `combined_sd` from −0.33910966 to −221.45336; `proportional` with tlag = 0.5 − 10^−j falls from 107.99421 to −121.45336. With the first two samples swapped (a zero after a nonzero one, tlag at the time of the zero), `combined` rises from 1438.0598 to 1.4424234e+101. With tlag = 0.4 (an upper bound below t0) and a = 10^−j, `combined` stays finite and equals 107.99421 at j = 50. `pk1.oral_0_lag` (with `dur` fixed) behaves the same way (`combined`: −2.6429655 at j = 1, −222.00450 at j = 50).

**Order of the checks.** This check is the first one made, before `NonPositiveSigma` (FIT-ML-14b) and before any fitting.

**The sentences.** At the dose time (row 1): "The {error model} error model has no maximum likelihood here: observation(s) {rows} (time {t}, ...) are exactly 0 where the model predicts exactly 0 for every parameter value, so the standard deviation can shrink to 0 there without cost. Exclude those observations, or use the additive error model." With a lag (row 2): "The {error model} error model has no maximum likelihood here: observation(s) {rows} (time {t}, ...) are exactly 0 before the first nonzero observation, and with a lag the model predicts exactly 0 up to `tlag`, which is, or which the fit can move to, at or beyond those times, so the standard deviation can shrink to 0 there without cost. Exclude those observations, bound or fix `tlag` below the first of them, or use the additive error model." The oracle checks the words "time 0" (row 1) or "time 0.5" and "lag" (row 2), together with "exclude" and "additive".

**The refusal can come in two rounds, and this is accepted.** When a profile on a route with a lag has a zero at t = 0 and also a later leading zero, the rows at t = 0 are reported first, and the lag rows only on the next try, once the user has excluded the first ones. The two refusals are not merged because the remedies differ: bounding or fixing `tlag` cannot help a row at t = 0, while it can help a later leading zero (T-149 review, confirmation of 0e84e68, 2026-11-07).

**Not decided here.** Leaving the rows out by the fit itself, so that `combined` fits Theoph with the sample at t = 0, is a design choice for Abdou: open item OF-22. The `pk3.*` routes are not named by this version of the rule; the engine's list of routes with a silent dose time and its list of lag parameters must cover them when brick 2 is merged with them (T-149 thread, 2026-11-07).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: `errors.json` (the 18 rows `ml6e_*`: nine Theoph subjects, both combined forms; the 10 rows `ml6f_*` and their 15 controls that the engine must not refuse with this code, among them an upper bound on tlag below t0, a zero after a nonzero sample, a fixed tlag below t0, and a fixed tlag or a lower bound at or above t_nz), `ml6f_lag_paths.json` (the 256-bit paths above) and `scripts/ml_lag_unbounded.R`. The engine's tests are in `oracle_fit_ml.rs` and `tests_ml.rs`.
- Sources: D (the limit a → 0); the orchestrator's decisions of 2026-11-07 on T-148 (Q2) and on T-149 (A1 and its refinement, 0e84e68); the T-149 review (the two rounds).

### FIT-ML-13d Collapse of a combined model
This was property (c) of FIT-ML-13. T-151 (2026-11-07) renamed it FIT-ML-13d so that FIT-ML-13c names the unbounded likelihood, as the code and the oracle cite it. Code comments and R scripts written before that date still cite the collapse as FIT-ML-13c: `crates/caladrius-fit/src/ml.rs` (the comment in the exact-fit check), `crates/caladrius-fit/tests/oracle_fit_ml.rs` (two comments), `oracle/scripts/ml_collapse_check.R` (first line) and `oracle/scripts/fit_ml.R`.

- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the combined cases that end `at_bound` (`ml_f_combined`, `ml_f_combined_sd`, the Indometh and Theoph combined cases), the collapse test of `oracle_fit_ml.rs` and `oracle/scripts/ml_collapse_check.R`.

### FIT-ML-14 Refusals before fitting: too few observations, and a standard deviation that is not positive
The run stops before the first iteration, with an error that names what to change (`AGENTS.md` section 7), in these cases.

(a) **N ≤ P + Q** (`TooFewObservations`). N counts the observations actually used (after the exclusions of FIT-DAT-01), P the free model parameters, Q the error parameters of the chosen model. Sentence: "The maximum-likelihood fit with the {error model} error model estimates {P + Q} parameters ({P} of the model and {Q} of the error) from {N} observations. It needs more observations than parameters. Fix a model parameter, choose an error model with fewer parameters, or add observations." The oracle fixes two phrases of it, `{P + Q} parameters` and `from {N} observations` (for example "4 parameters" and "from 4 observations", ML6c), and **no error model id**: the spec does not promise the id in the sentence, and the engine writes the word "chosen" in the place of {error model} when it does not have it (the oracle review of T-148, finding 5, and the orchestrator's decision on it, 2026-11-07). This is stricter than FIT-ERR-01 (N < P), because the error is estimated too: with N = P + Q the fit leaves no degrees of freedom for the error. Example ML6c.

(b) **σ_i not strictly positive or not finite at the starting point** (`NonPositiveSigma`). It happens with the proportional model when a prediction is exactly 0 at the start: an observation at the dose time t = 0 of a route that is not a bolus (ML6b), an observation at a time that the starting `tlag` of a lagged route has not passed (the model predicts 0 for t ≤ tlag), or a prediction that is not finite. Sentence: "The proportional error model gives a standard deviation of 0 at the observation at time {t} (the model predicts {ŷ} there). A proportional error cannot describe an observation where the prediction is 0. Leave that observation out, or use the additive or the combined error model." The combined models do not fail here: their starting values have a > 0 (FIT-ML-10). During the iteration the same condition on a trial point is not an error but a rejected trial (FIT-ML-07). **Where FIT-ML-13c comes first.** FIT-ML-13c is checked before this error and before fitting, so a zero observation that the model can only predict as 0, or that a lag can reach (FIT-ML-13c, row 2), is `unbounded_likelihood` and not `NonPositiveSigma`, under `proportional` as under the combined forms for the lag case. An observation at t = 0 with y ≠ 0 under `proportional`, as the Theoph sample 0.74 of subject 1 (ML6b), stays `NonPositiveSigma`. Example ML6b.

(c) Options: an unknown `error_model`, a missing `error_model` with `objective = ml`, an `error_init` entry ≤ 0 or of the wrong length, or a `weighting` other than `uniform` (FIT-ML-01) is `InvalidOption`, naming the option. An unknown value of `objective` is refused in the same way. The oracle fixes that each sentence contains the name of the field, the words `error_model` (cases `ml6d_unknown_error_model` and the missing one) and `objective` (`ml6d_unknown_objective`): a deserialiser's own "unknown variant" message does not contain them (T-148 thread, 2026-11-07). The engine's sentences are "option `error_model`: unknown error model `{id}`; use `additive`, `proportional`, `combined` or `combined_sd`" and "option `objective`: unknown objective `{id}`; use `wls` (least squares, the default) or `ml` (maximum likelihood)".
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: design choice; golden rule 6 and section 7 of `AGENTS.md` (readable errors); FIT-ERR-01; D (a standard deviation of 0 gives an infinite term in −2LL).

### FIT-ML-15 Refusals and statuses: an exact fit, and an error parameter on its bound
**Exact fit** (`ExactFit`). If the uniform least-squares fit of FIT-ML-10 leaves RSS ≤ 1e-26·Σ y_i² (the exact-fit test of FIT-CNV-01), or, during the iteration, the moved variable of a one-parameter error model (A for `additive`, B for `proportional`) falls to 1e-26 times its natural scale (Σ y²/N for A, 1 for B), the likelihood has no maximum (FIT-ML-09c) and the run stops without committing the iterate. The fit status is `exact_fit`, a status of its own (FitStatus `ExactFit`; T-149, fix round, 2026-11-07: before that, an exact fit found during the iteration was reported as `non_finite`), and `run` and `run_model` return the error `ExactFit` with the code `exact_fit` (the code of the oracle's refusal rows, ML6a). The uniform least-squares fit that refuses first is the case that real data reach; the mid-iteration case is not reachable from real data and is exercised by an engine unit test that forces the step. Sentence: "The model reproduces the observations exactly (the residual sum of squares is 0 within rounding). The maximum-likelihood estimate of the error does not exist: the likelihood grows without limit as the error standard deviation tends to 0. This usually means that the model has as many parameters as there are observations, or that the data are simulated without noise. Add observations, remove a parameter, or add noise to simulated data." For a two-parameter error model the same refusal applies when both components reach 0. Example ML6a.

**An error parameter on its bound.** A converged fit of `combined` or `combined_sd` can end with one component at exactly 0 (FIT-ML-13d). The status is `at_bound` (FIT-CNV-03), the component is reported as 0, its standard error is NC (reason `at_bound`), and the other statistics are those of the free block, conditional on the bound (FIT-BND-02); P + Q for the information criteria still counts both error parameters (FIT-ML-16). The text says: "The {additive|proportional} part of the combined error is estimated at 0: the data are described by the {proportional|additive} error model alone. Fit that model: it gives the same −2LL with one parameter fewer." A model parameter θ on a bound is handled as in FIT-BND-02. A fit is never presented as an ordinary converged fit in either case.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: design choice; FIT-CNV-01 (the exact-fit threshold), FIT-BND-02 (the conditional free block), FIT-CNV-03; D (the collapse, FIT-ML-13d).

### FIT-ML-16 Outputs: −2LL, AIC and BIC next to the WRSS forms
For `objective = ml` the result holds: `m2ll` = −2LL at the estimate (FIT-ML-04), and

- AIC_ML = −2LL + 2·(P + Q),
- BIC_ML = −2LL + (P + Q)·ln N,

with P the free model parameters and Q the number of error parameters of the chosen model, **including a component held on its bound** (the structure of the model is what is penalised; an estimate that landed on 0 was still estimated). N is the number of observations used. Both are NC when −2LL is not finite. The forms of FIT-OUT-07 (`aic`, `sbc`, N·ln WRSS + 2P and + P·ln N) are **still computed and shown, with uniform weights at the ML estimate, and labelled as the least-squares forms**; they never replace the likelihood forms and the two kinds are never compared with each other. They differ by an amount that depends on the data except for the additive model, where (D)

AIC_ML − AIC_WRSS = N·(1 + ln(2π/N)) + 2  and  BIC_ML − SBC_WRSS = N·(1 + ln(2π/N)) + ln N.

Example ML1: 8.1421958 and 7.7516337 (N = 5). The comparison of fits across error models (by AIC_ML or BIC_ML) is allowed only for the same observations and the same model parameters. Whether another program's reported objective contains the constant N·ln(2π) is not settled by the sources read: do not compare the value of −2LL with another program's objective without that check (OF-20).
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: S-27 (Akaike's criterion), S-29 (Schwarz's criterion), both as principles with the form −2 log-likelihood plus a penalty, which is the reader's reading; D (the additive identity); FIT-OUT-07; H24, H25.

### FIT-ML-17 Covariance, standard errors, correlations: expected information
**Covariance.** Cov(ζ̂) = I(ζ̂)⁻¹ over the free estimates, I the expected information of FIT-ML-06 at the estimate (the matrix 𝒥ᵀ𝒥 the last iteration already holds). The error parameters are reported in the user's units: for a = √A, SE_a = SE_A/(2a) and the covariances with the other estimates are scaled by 1/(2a) (the delta method); `combined_sd` needs no conversion. SE_j = √Cov_jj, CV%_j = 100·SE_j/|estimate_j| (NC at 0), and the correlation matrix is that of the covariance in these units, over all P + Q estimates; its eigenvalues and condition number are as in FIT-OUT-05. NC reasons: `singular` (a zero eigenvalue of 𝒥ᵀ𝒥), `at_bound` for a component on its bound, and `not_converged` is shown with the statistics flagged (FIT-CNV-03).

**Why expected and not observed information.** (1) It needs no second derivatives of the model: the same Jacobian as the iteration. (2) It is already computed. (3) It is positive semi-definite by construction; the observed information (minus the Hessian of the log-likelihood) can be indefinite away from the optimum. (4) For the additive model its θ block gives the hand check of FIT-ML-11 (least-squares covariance times DF/N). The price: the two differ, by 1 % to 27 % of the expected standard error in the values listed here (largest for the rate constant of Indometh subject 1 and for the additive part of the seed-1 profile), more when N is small. Values from the oracle (T-148, 2026-11-07; expected, then observed): the F data, proportional (`ml_f_proportional`): SE(V) 0.22199947 against 0.21922654; seed 1, combined (`ml_iv_s1_combined`): SE(a) 0.029123991 against 0.024103080 and SE(k) 0.0059912667 against 0.0061081294; Indometh subject 1, proportional (`ml_indometh_proportional`): SE(V) 5.3218731 against 4.6699842 and SE(k) 0.046290308 against 0.033788709. The observed form is half the Hessian of −2LL at 256 bits on the moved variables, inverted over the free block and carried to the user's units by the delta method (the `observed_information` entry of the options files); it is stored in the oracle as `se_obs.*` and `correlation_obs.*` beside the expected `se.*` and `correlation.*`. It is offered later as an option (`information`, OF-16). The values printed for the observed form in the first version of this section came from `optimHess` (central differences) in the prototype and differ from the oracle's by 4.5e-5 relative on the F data (T-148 thread, 2026-11-07) and by up to 0.3 % on Indometh subject 1; the oracle's values replace them.

**Brick 3.** The population brick is not specified here; the working assumption is that it reuses this definition (expected information on the stacked system) for the block of fixed effects and residual error whenever its objective is conditionally Gaussian, and adds the observed form if the population oracle needs it. No source read fixes what open population tools report (S-44 gives the arguments of `saemixData` and `saemixModel` only); this is a design choice to be revisited when brick 3 is specified.

No confidence interval is defined in this version: the Student interval of FIT-OUT-03 has no degrees of freedom in a maximum-likelihood fit with estimated variance, and the choice between a normal quantile and a Student quantile with N − P − Q degrees of freedom is open item OF-21. The planar interval of FIT-OUT-04 is not offered for `ml`.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: D (the covariance as the inverse of the information of the log-likelihood; the delta method); design choice for expected over observed and for brick 3; H30 (R: expected from the stacked system; the first observed values by `optimHess`, now replaced); the oracle files `ml_f_proportional.csv`, `ml_iv_s1_combined.csv`, `ml_indometh_proportional.csv` (`se.*`, `se_obs.*`).

### FIT-ML-18 IWRES and its summaries
At the estimate, for each observation used: σ_i = g(ŷ_i; ξ̂) and **IWRES_i = (y_i − ŷ_i)/σ_i = z_i**. The residual table of FIT-OUT-09 gets two columns for `ml`, `sigma` and `iwres`, in the place of `weight` and the weighted residual (those are undefined here). Summaries over the N observations used: the mean, the standard deviation with divisor N − 1, the number with |IWRES| > 2 and the number with |IWRES| > 3 (strictly greater). By FIT-ML-13, Σ z_i² = N at a converged fit, so the standard deviation is not a free diagnostic: it is close to 1 by construction, and for the proportional model with V a scale the mean is 0 too (examples ML2, ML4). What an analyst reads is the pattern of the IWRES against time and prediction and the counts, which for a normal model are expected to be about 4.5 % and 0.3 % of N. Rows left out (missing, BLQ, FIT-DAT-01) have no IWRES (shown empty, not counted). **NC** when: the fit has not converged (shown with the "not converged" mark, not hidden), σ_i is not finite, or fewer than 2 observations are used (the standard deviation). The IWRES here is the one of an individual fit; the population quantities with random effects (brick 3) are a different thing and keep their own names.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: the definition given by card T-147; D (Σ z² = N). No source read in full defines the name IWRES, so the rule is not tagged `documented`; H24 to H27 (the IWRES lists of the examples).

### FIT-ML-19 BLQ stays discarded; censored likelihood is not specified
Observations below the limit of quantification are left out of the likelihood exactly as FIT-DAT-01 says for least squares: counted (`n_blq`), shown in the table, not used. When there are many of them the estimates of the error and of the terminal phase can be biased; the user is told the count. The method that keeps them, a likelihood term for the probability of being below the limit (called M3 in the censoring literature of the open tools), is **later**, as an item for brick 3 (OF-21), not a rule: no formula for it is given here. The data columns that carry a censoring flag and a limit are specified in `specs/data.md` (DAT-CEN-*) so that the table does not change when the method is added.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: FIT-DAT-01 (same decision); S-46 (Beal 2001, seven ways of treating BLQ data, through the Europe PMC record summary: discarding costs little when rare and replacing by zero is poor; paper not read); S-43 (M3 and M4 named in the censoring vignette of `nlmixr`, summary only).

### FIT-ML-20 Contents of the result, the trace, and the flags
The result of `objective = ml` contains: `objective`, `error_model`, the estimates of θ and of the error parameters in the user's units with their SE, CV% and (for the pair) the correlation (FIT-ML-17), `m2ll`, `aic_ml`, `bic_ml`, the least-squares forms of FIT-OUT-07 (labelled, FIT-ML-16), `n_used`, `n_missing`, `n_blq` (who supplies them: see below), the status of FIT-ML-08, the residual table with `sigma` and `iwres`, the IWRES summaries, the curve of FIT-OUT-10, the partial derivatives of FIT-OUT-08 (of θ only) and the trace. **Trace**: one row per iteration including iteration 0 (the start chosen by FIT-ML-10): iteration, −2LL, θ, the error parameters in the user's units, the step factor ν, λ, the number of halvings, the decrement Δ; the last row carries the status. The least-squares fit that produced the start is not part of the trace but its estimate is reported as the `start`. **Flags** (FIT-FLG-01) apply with DF = N − P − Q for "few degrees of freedom", the correlation and condition flags over all P + Q estimates, and two additions: *at bound* for an error component, and *slow convergence* when more than 50 iterations were used (informational; it does not make the fit "not converged"; the polish rows of FIT-ML-08 are not counted).

**Who supplies `n_used`, `n_missing` and `n_blq`.** The fit crate does not produce them: it receives cleaned rows, because the missing and BLQ rows are left out before the fit (FIT-ML-01, FIT-DAT-01), so it knows N but not what was left out. The layer that holds the raw rows supplies the three counts: the dataset of `specs/data.md` (the observation statuses of DAT-REC-01 and the censoring of DAT-CEN-01, with `n_used` = the observations that reach the fit), carried to the result by the routing of the dataset into the analyses (DAT-USE-02). Until that routing exists the engine's run layer computes them from the table, as it does for the least-squares fit. Decision of the orchestrator on the T-149 thread, 2026-11-07; the routing is a later card.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: FIT-OUT-11 and FIT-FLG-01 (the layout carried over); design choice; `AGENTS.md` sections 4 and 7; T-149 thread (the gap and its decision).
