   - the three known-answer vectors first, then the words of SX1 and the big-seed words.
2. **The transform (SIM-ORA-02):**
   - AS 241 from the table of the spec, against a 256-bit inverse (`Rmpfr`).

3. **The exact part (SIM-ORA-03):** given z, the values of η, P, IPRED, σ and y. The engine side is a test entry point that takes z^η and z^ε per subject.
4. **The distribution (SIM-ORA-04):** assert the stated bounds and record the statistics (SX3).
5. **Invariance (SIM-ORA-05):** threads, other subjects and the replicate prefix.
6. **The round trip with the fit (SIM-ORA-06):** recorded as aggregates only. It cannot run until T-154, so it is written and marked to wait.
7. **The refusals of section 7**, each with a control. Include:
   - `duplicate_times`;
   - a random effect on `tlag` or `dur`;
   - empty `random_effects`;
   - a seed as a decimal string, small and above 2⁵³;
   - `label_key_collision`, if a pair of labels can be found cheaply (otherwise say that it cannot be tested);
   - a `pk3.*` id, which is `InvalidOption` (m11).
8. **The fixed rules:** negative DV with `keep`, BLQ at the LLOQ, the `{rep}:{label}` export, and the type 7 quantiles of SX5.

## Rules from the review

- **Bit-exact asserts** only on the known-answer vectors, the counters, the words, the uniforms, L and the central-region z.
- **Tolerance asserts** on the tail z, η, P, IPRED, σ and y.
- **Measuring the tolerances:** the tolerances are measured, not guessed (OS-05). Put each proposal in your report with its measured margin; the orchestrator files them in `board/QUESTIONS.md`.
- **Exchanging doubles:** doubles go between R and Rust as hex floats (`sprintf("%a")`) or 64-bit patterns. R never reads a decimal written by another tool as a bit-exact value: `as.numeric("2.58859887947042")` is one ulp off in R 4.5.2. Rust may read R's decimals.
- **Comparisons:** parsed values, not strings, since R drops a trailing zero in `%a`.
- **R's generator:** record `RNGkind()` wherever R simulates.
