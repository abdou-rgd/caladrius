## Goal

Write `specs/population.md`: a contract precise enough that an oracle agent and an engine agent can work from it without seeing each other's work. It covers one estimator, FOCE with interaction (FOCEI), with FO as its special case at η = 0 (`AGENTS.md` section 1). Every rule gets an id (`POP-*`). Every formula cites its source, with the page or equation number. A number enters the spec only from a cited source or an oracle file; none comes from memory.

1. **The model:**
   - fixed effects θ;
   - random effects η_i ~ N(0, Ω), log-normal on the structural parameters (P_i = θ·exp(η_i));
   - which parameters may have no η;
   - the shape of Ω: diagonal, and whether one full block is in v1. Propose, with the reason.
   - The structural models are the `pk1.*` and `pk2.*` ids. Say whether `pk3.*` enters v1.
2. **The residual error:** additive, proportional and combined. Use the forms and names of the ML fit (`specs/fit.md` FIT-ML-02 and 03), evaluated on the individual prediction, which is the interaction.
3. **The objective:**
   - the FOCEI approximation of −2 log-likelihood around the conditional modes η̂_i, written out per subject;
   - the FO case at η = 0;
   - **the OFV convention, named:** with or without the constant n·ln(2π). The answer of every command states which one (an idea of the Pumas note: a value without its convention cannot be compared);
   - the inner problem, the EBE: the individual objective over η_i, which is the ML objective of brick 2 plus η'Ω⁻¹η. Say how it reuses `caladrius-fit`;
   - its refusals, reusing the `unbounded_likelihood` rules of FIT-ML-13c where they apply.
4. **The estimation:**
   - the outer parameterisation (θ on the log scale or not, Ω through its Cholesky factor or log variances, σ);
   - bounds;
   - the outer optimiser and its stopping rules;
   - **two-stage initial estimates:** one ML fit per subject, then means and variances. These belong in T-154.
   - The tests compare estimates with the open oracle within a method tolerance. Say what that tolerance is meant to cover. The number itself goes to `board/QUESTIONS.md` through the orchestrator (`AGENTS.md` section 5).
5. **The covariance of the estimates:** which matrix is the default (R⁻¹, S⁻¹ or R⁻¹SR⁻¹), how its derivatives are taken, and what "not computable" looks like, as in FIT-ML-17. Then SE, RSE and correlations.
6. **The diagnostics:**
   - PRED, IPRED, IWRES and CWRES, with the CWRES definition from its paper;
   - η-shrinkage and ε-shrinkage, from their paper;
   - AIC and BIC, with the number of parameters counted.
7. **The data:**
   - The input is the population dataset of `specs/data.md` (DAT-*): per-subject doses and observations.
   - BLQ: say what v1 does, for example exclusion (M1) only, and leave M3 as an open item.
   - Constant covariates are read but not modelled in v1; covariate models are a later card after T-155.
8. **Determinism:** the same answer, bit for bit, on 1 thread, on N threads and in wasm. Say which reductions this constrains, such as the order of the sum over subjects. This is an idea of the Pumas note: a digest test across the three.
9. **What the oracle can test,** in this order:
   - **(a)** Cases where the Laplace approximation is exact: a model linear in η with additive error, where FO = FOCE = the exact marginal likelihood in closed form. They can be computed at 256 bits with no population software at all.
   - **(b)** The OFV at fixed parameters against nlmixr2. Say how nlmixr2 evaluates the objective at given parameters without estimating, from its own documentation.
   - **(c)** Estimates against nlmixr2, with saemix and `nlme` as second looks.
   - **(d)** **Sealed simulate-then-fit tests** (the Pumas note): data simulated in R from known parameters with a recorded seed, hashed and committed before any engine code exists, then a check that the parameters are recovered within a method tolerance.
10. **Open items for Abdou,** each marked as such, never written as a rule.
