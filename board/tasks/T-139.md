## Brief

**Goal.** Caladrius computes what a pharmacometrician expects from a PK engine, from the command line and over MCP, with today's guarantees: determinism, a seal under every answer, an independent oracle, and no figure claimed without its test. Four bricks, in this order, each on its own cards:
1. **Three compartments** (`pk3.*`): IV bolus, infusion, zero- and first-order absorption with and without lag, multiple dosing and steady state, closed forms and partial derivatives, at the level of `pk1.*` and `pk2.*` (registry, conformance, `describe()`).
2. **What individual fitting still lacks.** The orchestrator's inventory is in the thread of 2026-11-07; Abdou picks the list before any card of this brick is written.
3. **Population PK (NLME).** One estimator, FOCE with interaction (FOCEI), with FO as its special case at η = 0. Log-normal random effects, additive, proportional or combined residual error, OFV, variance-covariance matrix of the estimates, individual estimates (EBE), then the diagnostics (PRED, IPRED, IWRES, CWRES, shrinkage). The VPC and NPDE need brick 4 and come after it if time allows. Open oracles only: nlmixr2 first, saemix and `nlme` as second looks, never a commercial program (rule 1). Every conformance figure goes through `cargo xtask conformance`.
4. **Population simulation** from estimated parameters, with a seeded generator written in the engine. It makes the test sets and the scenarios of the future wave 4 of T-130 (S3, a fit of minutes).

Why these models. Sonnet at high effort for the code of the clear cards, as Abdou asked, although `AGENTS.md` section 8 names Opus for `engine`: the pk3 closed forms follow the pk2 pattern and a 256-bit oracle catches a wrong digit. Opus at high effort for T-153 and T-154 only: the FOCEI objective and its covariance are the hardest derivations of the iteration, and the final estimates are checked within a method tolerance, so a subtle error is less certain to be caught. `oracle` is always a new agent that reads only `specs/` and the R side. `reviewer` on Fable reads a diff and its card, never a crate, one at a time; it also reads `specs/population.md` before T-152 and T-153 start, because a wrong formula there would reach both the engine and our own naive check.

Budget, an estimate and not a measure: the Sonnet cards of iteration 2 took 75,000 to 199,000 tokens and 11 to 35 minutes each, the Opus card 181,000 tokens and 26 minutes (`board/LOG.md` and the orchestrator's notes on T-131 to T-137). About 22 cards and 8 Fable reviews gives an order of 3 to 5 million subagent tokens, over several quota windows.
