### 2. The reader's open point: "no quantity is negative when tested"

It is true on the macro path. Read in the code order:

- **Inputs.**
  - Before `from_macro` (`params3.rs:219`, `check(.., false)`) each of the six macro values (a, b, c, α, β, γ) must be finite and > 0.
  - The dose must be finite and ≥ 0 (`:240-241`), and `from_macro` refuses 0 (`:361`).
- **Quantities handed to `derived`.**
  - a+b+c, vc, n1, n0 and α·β·γ are sums or products of positive numbers.
  - The double-double sums of positive terms stay positive, and the fallback is the same.
  - k10 is a quotient of positives.
  - The gap is never tested by `derived`. It is `sqrt(delta)/t` or `0.0` (never negative), and it is checked by `is_finite()` (`:376`) and `<= 0.0` (`:383`).
  - k21 and k31 are positive.
  - The offsets are `.abs()` (`:405-406`).
  - gap×pole, the quotient, and cl, q2, vp2, q3 and vp3 are products and quotients of positives.
- A NaN or an infinity does reach `derived`, but a NaN is not negative. The sentence is therefore a fact about the code, not only a reading. It depends on the domain checks of items 1 and 2, so I suggest the clause "(the domain checks have made every input finite and positive)". That is not required.
